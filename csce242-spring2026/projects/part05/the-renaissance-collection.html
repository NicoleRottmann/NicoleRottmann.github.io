The Renaissance

The Renaissance, spanning roughly the 14th to 17th centuries, marked a cultural rebirth in Europe grounded in humanism, scientific inquiry, and a renewed interest in classical antiquity. Artists shifted their focus toward realism, proportion, anatomy, and perspective, striving to represent the human figure and natural world with unprecedented accuracy and harmony. Linear perspective, chiaroscuro (the use of light and shadow), and anatomical study became defining techniques of the period.

The era flourished across multiple mediums, including fresco, oil painting, marble sculpture, architecture, drawing, and engraving. Innovations in oil painting allowed for richer color and depth, while sculptors revived classical contrapposto poses and idealized forms inspired by ancient Greece and Rome.

Notable artists of the Renaissance include Leonardo da Vinci, known for his scientific approach to art and mastery of composition; Michelangelo, celebrated for monumental sculpture and expressive anatomy; Raphael, admired for balanced compositions and clarity; Sandro Botticelli, recognized for lyrical mythological scenes; and Titian, whose use of color shaped Venetian painting.

The Renaissance laid the foundation for Western art as we know it today, establishing principles of beauty, proportion, and intellectual depth that continue to influence artists centuries later.
