The Last Supper

Leonardo Da Vinci · Tempura and oil on plaster · c.1495-1498

The Last Supper (c. 1495–1498) by Leonardo da Vinci is one of the most celebrated and studied works of the Italian Renaissance. Painted on the wall of the refectory of the Convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan, the mural captures the dramatic moment described in the Gospel of John when Christ announces that one of his twelve apostles will betray him. Rather than depicting a static religious icon, Leonardo transforms the biblical narrative into a deeply human and psychological event.

The composition is meticulously structured using linear perspective, with all orthogonal lines converging at Christ’s head, making him the calm and spiritual focal point of the scene. The apostles are arranged in dynamic groups of three, each reacting with shock, disbelief, anger, or confusion. Their gestures and expressions create a rhythm of emotional tension across the table, contrasting sharply with Christ’s composed, centered presence. This balance between harmony and drama reflects the Renaissance pursuit of both intellectual order and human realism.
